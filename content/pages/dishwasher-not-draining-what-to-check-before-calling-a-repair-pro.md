Opening the dishwasher to find dirty water sitting in the bottom is frustrating, especially when you have a full sink of dishes waiting. The good news: the manufacturers say most drain problems come from a clog somewhere along the drain path, like the filter, the sump, the hose, the air gap, or the sink and garbage disposal. Those are things you can check yourself. This guide walks you through them in order, and tells you when to stop and call a technician.

> **Safety first.** Whirlpool says to turn off power to the dishwasher at the circuit breaker, not just the control panel, and to check that the display and lights are off. Then close the water supply valve under the sink, usually on the hot water line ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Dishwashers/Product_Info/Dishwasher_Product_Assistance/Dishwasher_Not_Draining)). If you smell burning, see damaged wiring, or the breaker keeps tripping, stop and call a qualified technician. **Do not use drain cleaner inside the dishwasher.** Whirlpool says drain-opening chemicals can damage parts and create safety hazards, and GE also says not to use it ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16240)). **Never put your hand into a garbage disposal.** GE warns that serious injury could occur. Samsung and LG both warn that the filter and drain area can have sharp edges or debris, like broken glass.

## Step 1: Is it really not draining?

- **A little water can be normal.** GE says that on models without an Ultra Fine filter, 1 to 2 cups of clean water covering the bottom of the tub is normal. On models with an Ultra Fine filter, some water in the filter area is normal, but it shouldn't cover the bottom of the tub ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16573)).
- **Did the cycle finish?** GE says if the door was opened mid-cycle, you may need to press Start again after closing it. If water pooled because the cycle never restarted, press Cancel or Drain to reset the dishwasher. GE also says the door must be fully closed and latched for the cycle to run, so check that no dish is blocking it ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/why-wont-my-dishwasher-drain-completely)).
- **Read the water level.** Whirlpool says water slightly above the filter screen usually means a partial clog in the filter, sump, or hose. Water up near the door opening suggests a severe blockage or a drain pump that isn't running.
- **Note any error code.** Samsung lists 5C, 5E, OC, and OE as drain or overflow codes ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10004498/)). LG says OE means the dishwasher isn't draining properly ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/how-to-fix-an-lg-dishwasher-that-is-not-draining--20155352254013)). For other brands and codes, check your user guide.

## Step 2: Bail out the standing water

With the power off, Whirlpool says to put towels in front of the dishwasher, use a cup to bail water into a bucket, and use a turkey baster or hand pump to get the water out of the sump (the low well near the filter). Pour the dirty water down a sink or toilet.

## Step 3: Clean the filter and the sump

This is the most common fix. Whirlpool says many "clogged dishwasher" complaints start with a filter that hasn't been cleaned, or food stuck in the sump.

- **Take out the filter.** On many Whirlpool models, you remove the lower rack, twist the upper filter counterclockwise, and lift it out, then lift out the lower filter if there is one. Samsung and LG filters also twist out counterclockwise on many models. Your user manual has the steps for your model ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10002330/)).
- **Rinse it.** Whirlpool says to rinse the filters under warm water with a soft brush and to avoid scrubbing pads that can damage the mesh. GE says hot, soapy water and a scrub brush work.
- **Check the sump.** With the filter out, Whirlpool says to shine a flashlight into the sump and look for food, labels, seeds, toothpicks, or glass. GE lists noodles, bones, and paper labels from jars as common blockers. Remove debris carefully.
- **Put it back right.** Whirlpool says to seat the lower filter flat and turn the upper filter clockwise until it locks. Samsung says missing or badly fitted filter parts can cause drain errors themselves.
- **No removable filter?** LG says some of its models have a self-cleaning filter instead, so there's nothing to clean there ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-dishwashers-troubleshooting-an-oe-error-code--1440686618796)). Samsung says to wipe out the drain area, then run an empty Normal or Quick cycle with detergent.

Then run a short rinse cycle. Whirlpool says to listen for the drain pump and check whether water is left in the tub.

## Step 4: Check the sink and garbage disposal

Most dishwashers drain into the kitchen sink plumbing, often through the garbage disposal, so a clog there can back up into the dishwasher.

- **Run the disposal.** GE and Whirlpool both say to run the garbage disposal to clear it. GE suggests running it before every dishwasher cycle.
- **Test the sink.** Samsung says to run water in the sink for about 30 seconds. If the sink doesn't drain, the problem is the home's plumbing, not the dishwasher, and you should call a plumber.
- **New disposal or new dishwasher?** Whirlpool, GE, Samsung, and LG all say to check that the **knockout plug** inside the disposal's dishwasher inlet was removed. Whirlpool calls a forgotten plug a common misdiagnosis of a clogged dishwasher, and says it blocks drainage completely ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Dishwashers/Product_Info/Dishwasher_Product_Assistance/Newly_Installed_Dishwasher_Not_Draining)). The disposal's installation instructions explain how to remove it.

## Step 5: Check the drain hose

- **Kinks and crushing.** LG says heavy things stored under the sink can crush or kink the drain hose, which is usually white or gray. Move them and straighten the hose. Samsung says the most common cause right after an install is the dishwasher sitting on its own drain hose and pinching it shut.
- **A high loop or an air gap.** Without one, Whirlpool, GE, and Samsung say dirty water can flow back into the dishwasher. GE says a high drain loop is at least 32 inches above the floor, attached to the underside of the countertop. LG's limits for its dishwashers: a hose no longer than 12 feet, with no extension added, and the hose end 12 to 40 inches above the floor. Local plumbing codes decide which setup you need, so follow your installation manual.
- **Old hose?** GE says new dishwashers come with a white corrugated drain hose that should be used, and that reusing an old, worn hose can lead to kinks or blockages.
- **Clearing a clogged hose.** Whirlpool says to put a towel and pan under the sink connection, loosen the clamp, detach the hose, and flush it with warm water. For a stubborn clog, use a flexible plastic line or brush, not sharp tools that could puncture the hose. Reattach it with the clamp snug but not over-tightened.

## Step 6: Clean the air gap (if you have one)

An air gap is the small cap, usually chrome, that sits on top of the sink next to the faucet. GE says to remove the cover, unscrew the plastic cap underneath, and clear out any buildup ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/why-is-my-dishwasher-clogged)). LG says to turn the dishwasher off first, use a toothpick or brush, and you can pour hot water into it with a turkey baster. Whirlpool says if water spills out of the air gap into the sink while the dishwasher drains, the blockage is further down, between the air gap and the drain.

## Step 7: Two more things to rule out

- **Too many suds.** GE says the wrong type of detergent, or too much of it, can make so many suds that the dishwasher can't drain properly. Use only dishwasher detergent.
- **A frozen drain line.** In cold weather, Samsung says to leave the cabinet under the sink open or warm the area, and call a plumber if the lines stay frozen. Samsung notes that frozen water or drain lines are not covered under its warranty service, so rule this out before booking a visit.

- [Turkey baster](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=turkey+baster&tag=applianceh06-20): Whirlpool suggests one for removing water from the sump (Step 2), and LG uses one to flush the air gap (Step 6).
- [Soft nylon brush](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=soft+nylon+cleaning+brush&tag=applianceh06-20): for scrubbing the filter mesh without damaging it (Step 3).
- [Dishwasher filter](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dishwasher+filter&tag=applianceh06-20): commonly replaced when the mesh is torn or damaged. Search with your model number.
- [Dishwasher drain hose](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dishwasher+drain+hose&tag=applianceh06-20): commonly replaced when the old hose is worn, kinked, or damaged. Check the length against your installation manual.
- [Dishwasher drain pump](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dishwasher+drain+pump&tag=applianceh06-20): Whirlpool names a jammed drain pump as a frequent cause. Search with your model number, and call a pro if you're not comfortable with electrical connections.

## When to stop and call a pro

- **It still won't drain** after you've cleaned the filter and sump, cleared the hose and air gap, and confirmed the sink drains. GE says service is needed at that point.
- **It hums but no water moves.** Whirlpool says a steady hum points to a jammed pump impeller or a blockage in the pump.
- **You hear no pump at all during the drain.** Whirlpool says that can mean a door switch, wiring, pump, or control board problem.
- **Grinding noises.** Whirlpool says this can mean hard debris, like glass, is in the pump area.
- **The drain hose is damaged** and can't be straightened. Samsung says to request service.
- **A drain code keeps coming back** after the checks. LG says a part may be faulty and to contact LG support.
- **Burning smells, damaged wiring, or a breaker that keeps tripping.** Stop and call a technician.
- **It's a portable GE dishwasher** connected to the sink with a unicouple. GE says those need service.
- **It's a brand-new install.** LG says an OE code on the first cycle points to a drain hose installation problem that the installer should fix.
- **It's still under warranty or a protection plan.** Check that before booking anyone else.

## What does it cost?

These are published averages, not quotes. Your price depends on your city, the part, and the diagnostic fee.

- **Whirlpool** lists average out-of-warranty dishwasher repairs at **$200 to $450** for parts and labor. It says these are 2020 averages from its W Service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York), and that actual costs vary ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html)).
- **Sears Home Services** says the average dishwasher repair can be **$150 to $200**, and that many dishwasher repairs cost less than $250. It's a repair company, so treat that as a vendor estimate ([Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/dishwasher-repair-service)).

Ask for a written estimate and whether the diagnostic fee counts toward the repair.

If the sink itself won't drain, call a plumber instead. Have your model and serial number ready. Tell the scheduler how much water is left in the tub, any error code, whether it hums or makes no sound when it should drain, whether anything was recently installed, and what you already tried. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.
