A dryer that tumbles but never gets warm is frustrating, and it's tempting to just run it again. Before you do, know that the most common causes are simple: the wrong cycle, a tripped breaker, a closed gas valve, or a clogged vent. That last one is also a fire risk, so it's worth checking first. This guide covers the safe checks you can do yourself and the signs that mean it's time to call a technician.

> **Safety first.** If you smell gas, don't touch any switches or try to light anything, and don't use a phone inside. LG says to get everyone out of the room or building and call your gas supplier. If you can't reach them, call the fire department ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-dryer-what-to-do-if-you-smell-gas-around-your-lg-dryer--20152469716816)). If you smell burning, see smoke, or the breaker keeps tripping, stop using the dryer and call a technician. Unplug the dryer (or turn off its breaker) before you pull it out or look behind it. The CPSC warns that lint can build up in the dryer or exhaust duct, block airflow, cause too much heat, and start a fire ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Guides/Home-Fire-Electronics-and-Electrical/Overheated-Clothes-Dryers-Can-Cause-Fires)).

## Step 1: Make sure it's really not heating

- **Do a quick heat test.** Whirlpool's test: choose a heated, timed cycle, run it empty for 5 minutes, then open the door and feel inside. If it's warm, the dryer is heating, and slow drying is more likely an airflow problem (see Step 4) ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Dryers/Product_Info/Dryer_Product_Assistance/Dryer_is_Not_Heating)).
- **Check the cycle.** Whirlpool says Air Only or Air Fluff cycles never add heat. GE says to make sure you picked a dry cycle, not fluff or cool down ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16921)).
- **Is it a heat pump (ventless) dryer?** Whirlpool says its HybridCare heat pump dryer dries at low temperatures, so clothes may feel cooler than you expect, and it uses little or no heat for the first 10 to 20 minutes ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Dryers/HybridCare_Dryer/Drying_Performance/Not_Heating_%2F%2F_Long_Dry_Times/Not_Heating_-_HybridCare_Dryer)). That's normal for that type.

## Step 2: Electric dryer? Check both breakers

Electric dryers need 240 volts. Whirlpool says they run on a separate 30-amp circuit with **two** breakers: one runs the motor that turns the drum, and the other runs the heating element. If only the heater side trips, the drum still turns but there's no heat.

- **Reset the breaker fully.** GE says a breaker can be partly tripped even when it doesn't look tripped. Turn the dryer's breaker all the way off, then back on, and test again. If your home has fuses, GE Factory Service says to check for blown fuses ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/dryer-troubleshooting-guide)).
- **Look for an error code.** On LG dryers, **nP** means no current is reaching the heater. LG says to turn off the dryer's breaker for at least 10 seconds, and if that doesn't work, have a licensed electrician check the outlet. If the dryer was just installed, call the installer to check the cord and outlet ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-dryer-error-code-list--20152310891742)).
- **Just installed or just moved?** Whirlpool and GE both say a wrong power cord hookup or low voltage can stop an electric dryer from heating. GE says to check, with the dryer unplugged, behind the cord cover on the back for burned, broken, or loose wires. If you see burn marks, GE says the cord and terminal block both need replacing, which is a job for a technician or electrician.

## Step 3: Gas dryer? Check the gas valve

- **Is the valve open?** Whirlpool says the drum of a gas dryer will still turn with the gas shut off. The valve on the supply line is open when the handle is parallel to the gas pipe.
- **Is it a new install?** GE says to make sure the gas line was turned on. A gas dryer also needs its own 120-volt outlet for the motor and controls ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16922)).
- **Code d80 on an LG gas dryer?** LG says that code can mean the dryer isn't getting enough gas, not just a clogged vent.
- **Gas on, still no heat?** GE says to contact service. Don't take apart gas parts yourself.

## Step 4: Clean the lint filter and check the vent

A clogged vent can make a dryer feel like it isn't heating. Samsung says some newer models turn the heat down when they sense a blocked vent, and cleaning the vent gets them heating properly again ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10004809/)).

- **Lint filter:** The U.S. Fire Administration says to clean it before and after each load, and never run the dryer without it ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/clothes_dryer_fire_safety_flyer.pdf)).
- **The hose behind the dryer:** USFA says to check that it isn't damaged, crushed, or restricted, and to clean the lint that builds up behind the dryer. NFPA recommends rigid or flexible metal venting ([NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/-/media/project/storefront/catalog/files/safety-tip-sheets/dryersafetytips.pdf)).
- **The outside vent:** With the dryer running, go outside and make sure the vent flap opens. NFPA adds that you should check it isn't covered by snow.
- **Run a vent test if you have one.** On Samsung models with a Vent Blockage test, codes like C8/C80 mean about 80% clogged and C9/C90 about 90% clogged. LG dryers show d75, d80, d90, or d95 for a duct that's 75% to 95% clogged.
- **No vent test?** Samsung's check: unplug, disconnect the vent from the dryer, plug back in, run a Time Dry cycle for at least 30 seconds, and feel for heat. If it's hotter with the vent off, the vent needs cleaning or doesn't meet the manual's requirements.

**How often to clean the vent:** NFPA says at least once a year, or more often if drying takes longer than normal. Samsung says once or twice a year, even if the dryer passes its vent test. Whirlpool says every one to two years. You can do it yourself or hire a dryer vent cleaning service.

## Step 5: Reset, but only after the checks

Samsung says to reset by unplugging the dryer (or turning off the breaker) for 1 to 5 minutes. But it warns not to reset first: a reset won't fix a clogged vent, and clearing the same error over and over erases the history a technician uses to diagnose the problem ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10004810/)). On Samsung dryers, heating codes like **HC** or **hE** usually mean a clogged lint screen or vent. On electric models they can also mean low voltage, and Samsung says an electrician should check that ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10001006/)).

## When to stop and call a pro

- **You smell gas or burning, or see scorch marks.** Turn it off and don't use it until it's been checked.
- **The breaker trips again** after you reset it, or the power cord or outlet looks burned.
- **Gas is on, breakers are fine, and the vent is clear, but there's still no heat.** GE Factory Service lists the heating element, thermostats, and timer as common causes. GE says a heating element can't be repaired, only replaced, and testing thermostats takes electrical expertise.
- **The same error code keeps coming back** after cleaning the lint screen and vent.
- **The vent can't be reached or cleaned** (for example, a long run through a wall or roof). That's a job for a vent cleaning service.
- **It's still under warranty or a protection plan.** Check that before booking anyone else.

## What does it cost?

This is a published average, not a quote. Your price depends on your city, the part, and the diagnostic fee.

- **Whirlpool** lists average out-of-warranty dryer repairs at **$200 to $350** for parts and labor. It says these are 2020 averages from its W Service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York) and that actual costs vary ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html)).
- **Sears Home Services** says a repair is usually worth it if it costs less than 50% of the price of a new dryer and the dryer is under 10 years old. It also says its diagnostic fee is credited toward the repair if you go ahead. It's a repair company, so treat that as a vendor's rule of thumb ([Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/dryer-repair-service)).

Vent cleaning by a separate vent service isn't included in these figures. Ask for a written estimate and whether the diagnostic fee counts toward the repair.

## How to find a repair service

Start with the brand. Its service line knows your model and warranty. These links were all checked on November 19, 2026:

- **Whirlpool:** [online scheduling](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html).
- **GE Appliances:** [schedule through Bodewell](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16875).
- **Samsung:** [repair request portal](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/service/), or call 1-800-SAMSUNG.
- **LG:** [repair request page](https://www.lg.com/us/support/repair-service/schedule-repair).
- **Any brand:** [Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/dryer-repair-service) says it repairs gas and electric dryers from most major brands, no matter where you bought them.

Have your model and serial number ready; your owner's manual shows where the label is on your model. Tell the scheduler whether it's gas or electric, any error code you saw, and what you already tried. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.
