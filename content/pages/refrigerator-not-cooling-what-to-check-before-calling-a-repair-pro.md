A fridge that is running but not cold is urgent, because food is at stake. This guide covers what to do first for the food, the checks you can safely do yourself in about an hour, and the signs that mean it is time to call someone.

> **Safety first.** Unplug the refrigerator (or switch off its circuit breaker) before you clean anything behind or beneath it. Do not touch water lines for the ice maker or dispenser. Never open or tamper with the sealed refrigerant system. If you smell burning, see sparks, or the breaker keeps tripping, unplug the fridge and call a technician.

## Step 1: Protect your food

Do this before any troubleshooting.

- **Keep the doors closed.** FDA and USDA guidance says an unopened refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/food-and-water-safety-during-power-outages-and-floods)). A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, or 24 hours if it is half full, if the door stays closed.
- **Use a thermometer, not your nose.** Refrigerator food should be at 40°F or below. Federal guidance is "never taste food to determine its safety" and "when in doubt, throw it out" ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage)).
- **Know the limits.** FDA says to discard perishable food held at room temperature for 2 hours or more. FoodSafety.gov's chart lists what to keep and what to discard, for example meat, milk, and cooked leftovers are discarded and hard cheeses and butter are kept.
- **Move the important food now.** Use a cooler with ice or a friend's fridge while you troubleshoot. Frozen food that still has ice crystals can generally be refrozen, according to the same guidance.

## Step 2: Make sure it is actually broken

Several manufacturers say a fridge can seem warm when nothing is wrong.

1. **Give it time after a change.** Whirlpool says to wait at least 24 hours between temperature adjustments, then recheck. Samsung says it can take up to 28 hours for a fridge to reach a newly set temperature if the doors stay closed ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Refrigeration/Full-Size_Refrigerators/All_Refrigerator/Operation/Temperature_Concerns/Not_Cooling), [Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10003479/)). This also applies to a newly installed fridge.
2. **Check the temperature setting.** Controls get bumped. Whirlpool says the factory "mid setting" should suit normal household use. Whirlpool's blog suggests keeping the fridge between 33°F and 40°F and checking both the fridge and freezer controls, since they often have separate controls ([Whirlpool blog](https://www.whirlpool.com/blog/kitchen/why-is-my-refrigerator-not-cooling.html)).
3. **Check for demo mode (Samsung).** Samsung says a display showing "OF OF" or "O FF" means Cooling Off (demo) mode, which you can switch off without a reset ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10006926/)).
4. **Was a lot of warm food added, or the door left open?** Whirlpool says it can take several hours to stabilize after you add a large amount of warm items, and that frequent door opening warms the fridge.

## Step 3: Free checks that fix many "not cooling" calls

- **Clear the air vents.** Whirlpool says food packages blocking the interior vents restrict airflow. Samsung recommends keeping food about 2 inches from the vents. Don't overpack: Samsung suggests keeping the fridge at least two-thirds stocked, while LG suggests it be no more than 70% full ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/if-your-lg-refrigerator-is-not-cold-enough--20155457094933)).
- **Check clearance around the fridge.** The fridge needs room to release heat. Whirlpool's blog gives typical clearances of ½ inch on the sides and 1 inch at the back, and Samsung says 2 inches at the top, back, and sides. Your owner's manual has the number for your model.
- **Check where it sits.** Whirlpool says its fridge is designed for locations from 55°F to 110°F and should not be next to an oven or other heat source. Samsung says a space that is too cold (below 55°F) can also hurt cooling. Garages are a common problem.
- **Clean the door gaskets.** Whirlpool's blog says dirty, damaged, or worn door seals let cold air leak. Wipe them with warm soapy water and see if the door closes snugly.
- **Check that it is level.** Whirlpool's blog lists an unlevel fridge as a possible cause. Place a level on the floor under it and shim if needed, per your manual.
- **Clean the condenser coils (unplug first).** Whirlpool says dust, pet hair, and grease keep the coils from working. For most models: unplug the fridge, remove the base grille, and use a vacuum with a soft brush on the grille and the area behind it, then replace the grille and plug it in ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Refrigeration/Full-Size_Refrigerators/Product_Info/Cleaning_and_Care/How_and_When_to_Safely_Clean_the_Condenser_Coils)). Whirlpool says some models don't need coil cleaning and some can only be cleaned by a certified technician, so check your manual first. It suggests every 3 to 6 months for homes with pets, heavy kitchen traffic, or construction dust.
- **Power-cycle it (Samsung's advice).** Samsung says to unplug the fridge or turn it off at the breaker for 2 minutes. The fridge may run a self-test on restart and show an error code if it finds a problem. An error code may mean it needs service, and Samsung says no other troubleshooting is needed in that case.

## Typical causes behind a fridge that won't cool

The free checks above cover settings, airflow, seals, and dirty coils. When they don't work, the cause is often a part that needs a technician. LG's support site lists a blocked evaporator or fan, frost buildup, a blocked drain line, and a faulty compressor among the causes of cooling problems ([LG](https://www.lg.com/in/support/product-support/troubleshoot/help-library/cs-CT52006627-20155295251040/)). Sears Home Services lists compressors, thermostats, and door seals as typical replacement parts. We can't tell you which one yours is from the outside, which is what the diagnostic visit is for.

## When to stop and call a pro

- **It is still warm after 24 to 28 hours** with correct settings, clear vents, and clean coils.
- **An error code shows** after a power cycle.
- **You smell burning, see sparks, or the breaker trips** when the fridge starts. Unplug it first.
- **You suspect the compressor or sealed system.** Federal rules under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act require technicians who service equipment that could release refrigerants to be certified ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-requirements)). This is not a DIY repair.
- **You have a warranty or protection plan.** Check the warranty page in your owner's manual (GE points owners to it) and call your plan provider before booking someone else.

## What does it cost?

Prices vary a lot by city and by repair, so treat these as rough published figures, not quotes.

- **Whirlpool** publishes average out-of-warranty repair estimates by appliance type. For refrigerators it lists **$230 to $360** for parts and labor. Whirlpool says these are 2020 averages from its service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York), and that they **exclude sealed-system repairs**. Whirlpool also says actual cost varies with location, diagnostic fees, and parts ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html)).
- **Sears Home Services** says the average refrigerator repair costs about **$220**, and that replacing a temperature sensor costs less while compressor replacement typically costs more. Sears is a repair company, so treat this as a vendor estimate ([Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/refrigerator-repair-service)).

We don't have a sourced price for sealed-system or compressor repairs, so we're not giving one. Ask for a written estimate, and ask whether the diagnostic fee is credited toward the repair. For repair versus replace, Sears's rule of thumb is that repair usually makes sense if the fridge is under 10 years old and the repair costs less than half of a new one.

Start with the maker of your fridge. The brand's service line knows your model and warranty status, and the links below are the real scheduling pages (all checked on November 18, 2026):

- **Whirlpool:** [online scheduling](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html) or [W Service](https://service.whirlpool.com/), 1-844-667-7221.
- **GE Appliances:** [schedule through Bodewell](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16875), or call 1-800-432-2737.
- **Samsung:** [repair request portal](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/service/), or call 1-800-SAMSUNG.
- **LG:** [repair request page](https://www.lg.com/us/support/repair-service/schedule-repair).
- **Any brand:** [Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/refrigerator-repair-service) says it repairs refrigerators regardless of brand or where you bought them.

Have your model and serial number ready, which are on a label inside the fridge. When you book, ask about the diagnostic fee, whether the visit is covered by a warranty or plan, and whether the technician carries common parts. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.
