A puddle under the washer feels like a disaster, but the manufacturers say most leaks come from simple things: a loose hose, a dirty door seal, too much detergent, or a house drain that can't keep up. The fix depends on **where** the water is coming from, so find that first, then work through the checks below.

**Make sure it's really the washer.** LG suggests running one rinse cycle with the tub empty. If water leaks during that cycle, request service. If nothing leaks during the rinse and spin, the leak may not be coming from the washer at all ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-front-load-washing-machine-troubleshooting-leaks--20155092730089)). GE also points out that floors can be uneven, so water from a leak at the back can show up at the front, and the other way around ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17531)). Dry the floor, then watch where fresh water first appears.

> **Safety first.** Water and electricity don't mix. If the washer is flooding, GE says to pause the cycle or turn the washer off at the control panel, or shut off its circuit breaker, and **not to unplug it while you're standing in water**. Once the power is off, turn off the water valves behind the washer ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17532)). If water has reached an outlet, a power strip, or an extension cord, leave it alone and turn off the breaker first. If you aren't sure the area is safe, call an electrician.

**Is it normal?** Samsung says some water may drip from a front-load washer's door after you open it. That's normal. It's only a leak if it drips while the door is closed ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10001007/)). LG says drops on the inside of the door after washing are leftover moisture from the spin cycle.

## Step 1: Leaking from the back? Check the hoses and the house drain

Both GE and Whirlpool say rear leaks are often hoses, installation, or household plumbing.

- **Fill (inlet) hoses.** Turn off the water first. Whirlpool says to check both ends of each hose, where it connects to the washer and to the house water supply. Both ends need a rubber hose washer, and the connections should be tight but not cross-threaded or over-tightened ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Front_Load_Washers/Leaking/Leaking_Inside_-_Front_Load_Washer)). GE says that if the connections are hand-tight but still leak, look for a missing or damaged rubber washer or a damaged hose. GE also says the fill hose screens and washers should be changed about every 5 years ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17531)).
- **Cracked hoses.** Look along each hose for cracks, kinks, punctures, or bulges. Whirlpool and Samsung both say damaged hoses should be replaced, and Samsung says to check the o-rings inside the hose ends too. Samsung also says to use brand-new supply hoses any time you install a washer, because old hoses can leak ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001021/)).
- **Hot and cold swapped?** On top-loaders, Whirlpool says hoses connected to the wrong hot and cold ports can cause leaking or condensation ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Top_Load_Washer/Water/Leaking/Leaking_from_Behind_-_Washer)).
- **Inlet filters.** LG says to pull the small filters out of the washer's inlet valves with needle-nose pliers, rinse them clean, and reconnect the hoses carefully. If the leak continues, replace the hose. If it still leaks from the house valve, call a plumber ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-washer-why-is-my-front-load-washing-machine-leaking--1431986512448)).
- **Drain hose depth.** Samsung says the drain hose should go 6 to 8 inches into the drain pipe, secured with a zip tie, and must not form an airtight seal. Too deep causes siphoning; too shallow and it can fall out and spill water on the floor. Don't remove the screw and holder that clamp the drain hose to the back of the washer ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10001007/)). GE also says a gap is needed between the hose and the standpipe to let air in.
- **The house drain.** Watch the standpipe while the washer drains or spins. GE says that if water backs up and overflows, often about three-quarters of the way through the spin, that points to a household plumbing problem that needs a plumber. Whirlpool says the drain must carry away at least 17 gallons (64 L) of water per minute ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Front_Load_Washers/Leaking/Leaking_from_Underneath_or_Bottom_-_Front_Load_Washer)). LG's test: pour one gallon of water into the drain. If it rises to the top and spills over, the drain is clogged.

## Step 2: Front-load washer leaking from the front? Check the door seal and dispenser

- **Clean the door seal (gasket).** GE and Whirlpool both say soil and soap left on the rubber seal can cause small leaks. Wipe it with a damp cloth, lift the edge, and clean the folds underneath ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17532)). Samsung says even a single strand of hair or a piece of thread can cause a leak.
- **Look for stuck items and damage.** Whirlpool says to check the seal after each use for clothing stuck in it and to make sure its drain holes aren't blocked. GE's factory service says a cracked or loose door seal has to be replaced, and to empty pockets because objects in them can damage the seal ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/why-is-my-washing-machine-leaking)). LG and Samsung say to request service if the gasket is torn.
- **Clean and close the dispenser drawer.** GE says a clogged dispenser blocks the ports into the drum, so the water backs up and overflows out of the washer. Clean the trays at least once a month; your owner's manual shows how. Make sure the drawer is fully closed.
- **Pods go in the drum.** GE and Whirlpool both say never to put detergent pods in the dispenser drawer. Put them in the drum before the clothes.
- **Don't overload.** Whirlpool says clothes outside the basket can get caught in the seal and stop the door from sealing. It also says an unlevel washer can leak ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Front_Load_Washers/Leaking/Leaking_from_Front_-_Front_Load_Washer)).
- **Pump filter cap.** If water is coming from the small access panel at the bottom front, LG says the drain pump filter and the residual water hose cap may not be closed securely. Samsung recommends cleaning the pump filter at least once a month or every 40 washes, whichever comes first, because a clogged filter can cause draining problems and leaks.

## Step 3: Top-load washer leaking from the front or underneath?

- **Too many suds.** GE's factory service says a leak from the front of a top-loader could be oversudsing from too much detergent ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/why-is-my-washing-machine-leaking)).
- **Overloading.** GE says an overstuffed drum can stop the washer from draining properly and cause leaks.
- **Adding clothes mid-cycle.** Whirlpool says adding clothes after the cycle starts can push the water level higher than expected, so water can flow over the inner tub and onto the floor. On the highest water level, add no more than one or two small items once the wash has started ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Top_Load_Washer/Water/Leaking/Leaking_Water_Underneath_-_Washer)).
- **Hoses and drain.** The back-of-washer checks in Step 1 apply to top-loaders too.

## Step 4: Soapy water or suds? Check the detergent

Every brand above lists too many suds as a leak cause.

- **Use HE detergent in HE washers, and less of it.** Whirlpool says non-HE detergent, or more than the recommended amount, makes excess suds that can cause leaks. GE adds that the softer your water, the less detergent you need.
- **Suds from the back.** Whirlpool and LG say excess suds can leak from the rear vent (LG calls it the safety air vent).
- **Skip "no splash" bleach.** Whirlpool says thickened "no splash" or "easy-pour" liquid chlorine bleach can make excess suds. Use regular liquid bleach.
- **Samsung LC, LE, 1E, or 1C code?** Samsung says these are water level or leakage codes. Look for a leak, check that the hoses behind the washer aren't kinked, then run an empty cycle with no detergent or additives. If suds appear, too much detergent or non-HE detergent was used. Keep running empty cycles until the suds are gone. If the code keeps coming back with no suds, request service ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10000997/)).

## Parts and tools that commonly help with this

These are links to Amazon search results, not specific products, so you pick the exact part. **Match any washer part to your washer's model number** (your owner's manual shows where the label is) and check it against the manual. **Turn off the power and shut off the water valves before you disconnect anything.** Buy a part only after the checks above point to it.

- [Washing machine fill hoses](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=washing+machine+fill+hoses&tag=applianceh06-20): commonly replaced when a hose is cracked, bulging, or leaking at the ends. Samsung says to use new hoses any time you install a washer.
- [Washer hose rubber washers](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=washing+machine+hose+rubber+washers&tag=applianceh06-20): the small seals inside each hose end. GE and Whirlpool say a missing or worn one causes leaks at the connections.
- [Front-load washer door gasket](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=front+load+washer+door+gasket&tag=applianceh06-20): commonly replaced when the seal is torn or cracked. Search with your model number; this repair is often easier to leave to a technician.
- [Washer drain pan](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=washing+machine+drain+pan&tag=applianceh06-20): a tray under the washer that GE's factory service suggests to help protect floors from leaks and overflows.
- [Water leak detector alarm](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=water+leak+detector+alarm&tag=applianceh06-20): sits on the floor and sounds an alarm when it gets wet, so you catch a slow leak early.

## When to stop and call a pro

- **The washer leaks during LG's empty rinse test.** LG says that means you should request service.
- **It overflows while filling.** GE says that points to the water valve or a pressure switch. Turn off the power and call for service.
- **You see oil under the washer.** GE says that likely means a transmission problem. Don't use the washer, and schedule service.
- **The door gasket is torn,** or the leak is coming from inside the cabinet after you've checked the hoses, seal, and dispenser.
- **The house drain backs up.** That's a plumbing problem, so call a plumber, not an appliance tech.
- **Water reached outlets, cords, or the floor under other appliances.** Leave the power off at the breaker and get an electrician to check it.
- **It's still under warranty or a protection plan.** Check that before booking anyone else. LG notes that if a leak turns out not to be a washer fault, service fees may apply even during the warranty period.

## What does it cost?

These are published averages, not quotes. Your price depends on your city, the part, and the diagnostic fee.

- **Whirlpool** lists average out-of-warranty washer repairs at **$215 to $385** for parts and labor. It says these are 2020 averages from its W Service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York) and that actual costs vary ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html)).
- **Sears Home Services** says the average washer repair costs **$150 to $350** depending on the problem, and lists leaking water among the problems it fixes. It's a repair company, so treat that as a vendor estimate. Sears also says repair is often more cost-effective than replacement if the washer is under 10 years old ([Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/washer-repair-service)).

A clogged house drain or a leaking house valve is a plumber's job, so it won't be covered by either estimate. Ask for a written estimate and whether the diagnostic fee counts toward the repair.

## How to find a repair service

Start with the brand. Its service line knows your model and warranty. These links were all checked on November 26, 2026:

- **Whirlpool:** [online scheduling](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html).
- **GE Appliances:** [schedule through Bodewell](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16875).
- **Samsung:** [repair request portal](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/service/), or call 1-800-SAMSUNG.
- **LG:** [repair request page](https://www.lg.com/us/support/repair-service/schedule-repair).
- **Any brand:** [Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/washer-repair-service) says it repairs most major washer brands no matter where you bought them.

Have your model and serial number ready. Tell the scheduler whether it's a front-load or top-load washer, where the water shows up (front, back, or underneath), when it leaks (filling, washing, draining, or spinning), any error code, and what you already tried. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.

If the washer leaves water in the tub instead of on the floor, see our guide to a [washer that won't drain](/washer-wont-drain-what-to-check-before-calling-a-repair-pro/).
