A dryer that won't start always seems to quit with a full load of wet laundry inside. Before you haul it to the laundromat, know that the manufacturers list several simple causes: a door that isn't fully latched, a Start button that needs to be held longer, a control lock, or a tripped breaker. This guide walks through those checks in order, then explains the parts that usually fail and when to stop and call a technician.

First, notice which kind of "won't start" you have, because the checks are different:

- **Nothing lights up at all.** Start with Step 2 (power). LG and GE both suggest opening the door first: if the drum light comes on, the dryer is getting power.
- **The lights or display work, but the drum won't turn.** Start with Step 1 (door, Start button, and settings).
- **It hums but the drum doesn't turn.** Turn it off and skip to "When to stop and call a pro."

> **Safety first.** Samsung says to switch off the circuit breaker before you touch the dryer's power cord ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10000730/)). Unplug the dryer (or turn off its breaker) before you pull it out or look behind it. If you see burn marks on the plug, cord, or outlet, stop and call a licensed electrician. If you smell gas, don't touch any switches or try to light anything, and don't use a phone inside. LG says to get everyone out of the room or building and call your gas supplier, and if you can't reach them, call the fire department ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-dryer-what-to-do-if-you-smell-gas-around-your-lg-dryer--20152469716816)). If you smell burning or see smoke, turn the dryer off at the breaker and don't use it until it's been checked.

## Step 1: Check the door, the Start button, and the settings

- **Is the door really latched?** Whirlpool says a door can look shut without being latched. Push on it to engage the latch. Some models have an upper and a lower latch, and both must be secure for the dryer to run. Check that no clothing or leftover packing material is caught in the door or blocking the latch ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Dryers/Dryer/Operation/Not_Starting/Not_Starting_or_Not_Operating_-_Dryer)).
- **Hold Start, don't tap it.** Whirlpool says to press and hold Start (or Push to Start, or Hold to Start) for two to five seconds; just tapping it may not give the motor enough time to engage ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Dryers/Product_Info/Dryer_Product_Assistance/Dryer_Not_Starting_or_No_Operation)). LG says its touch Start buttons are designed to activate only after a one-second press, and that the outer edges of the buttons aren't touch-sensitive, so press the center ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-dryer-button-the-poweroperation-button-is-difficult-to-press--20154720211622)).
- **Dry your hands.** Whirlpool and LG both say touch panels may not respond to wet hands or moisture on the panel. Use a fingertip, not a fingernail, and wipe the panel dry.
- **Turn off the control lock.** Whirlpool, Samsung, and LG all have a lock (sometimes called Child Lock) that stops the dryer from starting. On Whirlpool, **Loc** on the display means the control lock is on; hold the Control Lock key for 3 seconds to unlock it ([Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Dryers/Dryer/Error_Codes/Other_Error_Codes/Loc_-_Error_Code_-_Dryer)). Samsung says to hold the two labeled buttons for 3 or more seconds, or turn off the breaker for 60 seconds ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10007168/)). LG says most of its models unlock by holding the Child Lock button for 3 seconds. Your owner's manual shows the exact buttons.
- **Check the cycle.** Whirlpool says the knob must be on a dry cycle (Automatic Dry, AccuDry, or Time Dry on some models). The dryer won't start if it's set on the Wrinkle Shield or Wrinkle Reduction option.

## Step 2: Check the power (both breakers on electric dryers)

- **Is it plugged in all the way?** GE's first step is to make sure the plug is pushed completely into the wall outlet ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17381)). LG adds that some outlets are controlled by a wall light switch, so check that too ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/power-issues-laundry-dryer--20150394890820)).
- **Electric dryers use two breakers.** Whirlpool says electric dryers use two household fuses or circuit breakers (gas dryers use one), and to make sure both fuses are intact and tight or that neither breaker has tripped. If the problem continues, Whirlpool says to call an electrician.
- **Cycle the breaker fully.** Samsung says a tripped breaker can be hard to spot, and an electric dryer's circuit can be "half-tripped." Turn the breaker completely off for 30 seconds, then back on. If the breaker keeps tripping, Samsung says to contact a licensed electrician.
- **Gas dryer? Test the outlet.** Samsung says to plug in something else, like a lamp or phone charger. If the dryer has a drum light that comes on when you open the door, the outlet is working and you can skip this. GE says a dryer should be on its own dedicated circuit.
- **Just installed or just moved?** Whirlpool says electric dryers need a 240-volt supply and won't run correctly without it. With the dryer unplugged, GE says to look at the power cord at the back for a broken, burned, or loose connection. If the wires or terminal connections are burned, GE says both the cord and the terminal block need replacing, and Samsung says a visibly wrong or loose cord installation is a job for a licensed electrician or professional installer.

## Step 3: GE dryer showing a "1" or "2"?

On some GE models from 2019 and newer, GE says the dryer may need its "personality" setting chosen. When that happens, the dryer may not turn on or tumble, and a **1** or **2** shows on the display. Pressing Start switches between them: choose **1 for electric** dryers and **2 for gas** dryers ([GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17381)).

## Step 4: Look up any error code, then reset

- **Samsung door codes** (dC, dE, dF, do, d0) mean the door or latch isn't being detected as closed. Samsung says to check that the latch is secure and nothing is caught in the door. **Jammed-button codes** (bE, 6E and similar) mean a button is detected as stuck; Samsung says to power off and press and release every button on the panel ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10001006/)).
- **Confirm the cycle actually started (Samsung).** Select Time Dry, touch and hold Start, and watch the timer. If it doesn't count down after 1 to 2 minutes, the cycle didn't start; recheck the door and Child Lock. If the timer counts down but the drum doesn't turn, Samsung says to request service.
- **Reset (LG).** LG's hard reset: turn off the breaker, unplug the dryer, press and hold Power/Start for 5 seconds, then hold Play/Pause for 5 seconds, then plug it back in and turn the breaker on. If it still won't power on, LG says the dryer needs a repair visit.

## Step 5: Know the parts that usually cause it

If the checks above didn't help, GE's factory service lists the parts most often behind a dryer that won't start ([GE Appliances Factory Service](https://repair.geappliances.com/resources/faq/article/why-wont-my-dryer-start)):

- **Door switch.** If the dryer can't tell the door is closed, it won't do anything. GE's support page also says to open the door and look at the door switch: if it's scorched or pushed in, the dryer may not run.
- **Start switch.** The lights are on and the door switch is fine, but nothing happens when you press Start.
- **Thermal fuse.** A safety part that blows when the dryer gets dangerously hot, and it has to be replaced before the dryer will run again. GE says a blown thermal fuse is probably the result of a blocked exhaust vent, so clean the vent too, or the new fuse may blow as well. The U.S. Fire Administration says to clean the lint filter before and after each load and never run the dryer without it ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/clothes_dryer_fire_safety_flyer.pdf)).
- **Drive motor or belt.** If the dryer hums but doesn't work, GE says to check the drive belt for a break, wear, or slipping before suspecting the motor.
- **Control board.** GE says these rarely fail and are hard to test yourself; replacing one usually needs a technician.

GE suggests a multimeter to test the door switch, start switch, and thermal fuse for continuity. If you aren't comfortable working inside an appliance, that's the point to call a pro.

## When to stop and call a pro

- **You smell burning or gas, or see scorch marks** on the cord, outlet, terminal block, or door switch.
- **The breaker trips again** after you reset it. Samsung and Whirlpool say to contact an electrician.
- **The drum light is on, but the dryer won't power on** after the checks. LG says the unit needs a repair visit.
- **The timer counts down but the drum doesn't turn,** or the dryer hums without tumbling. Samsung says service is usually required, and GE points to the belt or drive motor.
- **The thermal fuse keeps blowing.** That points to a vent or overheating problem that needs to be found, not just another fuse.
- **The same error code keeps coming back** after a reset.
- **It's still under warranty or a protection plan.** Check that before booking anyone else.

## What does it cost?

This is a published average, not a quote. Your price depends on your city, the part, and the diagnostic fee.

- **Whirlpool** lists average out-of-warranty dryer repairs at **$200 to $350** for parts and labor. It says these are 2020 averages from its W Service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York) and that actual costs vary ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/repair/service.html)).
- **Sears Home Services** says a repair is usually worth it if it costs less than 50% of the price of a new dryer and the dryer is under 10 years old. It also says its diagnostic fee is credited toward the repair if you go ahead. It's a repair company, so treat that as a vendor's rule of thumb ([Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/dryer-repair-service)).

Ask for a written estimate and whether the diagnostic fee counts toward the repair.

Start with the brand. Its service line knows your model and warranty. These links were all checked on November 25, 2026:

- **GE Appliances:** [schedule through Bodewell](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16875).

- **LG:** [repair request page](https://www.lg.com/us/support/repair-service/schedule-repair).
- **Any brand:** [Sears Home Services](https://www.searshomeservices.com/repair/dryer-repair-service) says it repairs gas and electric dryers from most major brands, no matter where you bought them.

Have your model and serial number ready. Tell the scheduler whether it's gas or electric, whether it's completely dark or lights up but won't tumble, whether it hums, any error code, and what you already tried. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.
