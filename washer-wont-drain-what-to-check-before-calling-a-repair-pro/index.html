Washer won't drain: what to check before calling a repair pro

Last updated November 18, 2026

A washer that stops with water in the tub, or leaves your clothes soaking wet, is one of the most common laundry-day problems. Often the fix is a kinked hose or a clogged filter, not a broken machine. This guide covers the safe checks you can do yourself and the signs that mean it’s time to call a technician, or a plumber.

Safety first. Unplug the washer before you pull it out, take off the drain hose, or open the pump filter. If there is water on the floor near the outlet, turn off the washer’s circuit breaker instead of reaching for the plug. If you just ran a hot or sanitize cycle, Samsung says to wait about an hour for the water to cool to avoid burns (Samsung). Put towels and a shallow pan down first, because opening the filter or hose releases the water still inside. Never force a locked door open.

Step 1: Make sure it’s really stuck

  • Wait a few minutes. GE says it is normal for many models to pause for up to 3 minutes between parts of a cycle. If your washer has a dial, don’t turn it forward by hand during a pause, or you may skip the drain part of the cycle (GE Appliances).
  • Check the spin speed. Whirlpool says gentle, delicate, and handwash cycles spin slowly and leave more water in the load. A heavy-duty cycle spins faster (Whirlpool).
  • Re-balance the load. Whirlpool and GE both say an unbalanced or overloaded drum can stop the washer from spinning. Spread the wet items out evenly (or add a few items if it’s only one or two), then run Rinse & Spin or Drain & Spin.
  • Read the display. A drain error code tells you the washer knows it can’t drain. LG shows OE. Whirlpool front-loaders show F9 E1 for “long drain time” (Whirlpool). On GE top-loaders from 2015 and newer, a flashing Rinse light or “Po” means it took too long to pump out. GE says to press Start or choose Drain & Spin, and if it drains, the clog has probably cleared.
  • Is it a top-loader that sloshes but looks empty? GE says that sound is usually the balance ring, which holds liquid on purpose. That’s normal.

Step 2: If the door or lid won’t unlock

A front-loader usually stays locked while there is water inside. Whirlpool says the door may not unlock while the water is high, hot, or still moving, and that it can take up to five minutes after stopping before it unlocks. On some models, you press Pause/Cancel twice (Whirlpool). If it stays locked at the end of a cycle, Whirlpool’s advice is to unplug the washer for about 2 minutes so the controls reset, plug it back in, and run Drain & Spin. If the door still won’t open, call for service (Whirlpool). Some owner’s manuals describe a manual release, but Whirlpool says to try everything else first and drain the water before you open the door.

Step 3: Check the drain hose (most common cause)

Samsung says incorrect drain hose installation is the most common reason for drain problems, and that service is rarely needed. Pull the washer out gently (it’s unplugged, right?) and look at the hose behind it.

  • Kinks, crushing, or clogs. Straighten any kinks. LG says washer vibration can kink or pinch the hose. GE says you can unplug the washer, take off the hose over a bucket, and flush a clog out with a garden hose (GE Appliances Factory Service).
  • Not taped or sealed in. Whirlpool and Samsung both say the hose must not make an airtight seal with the standpipe. Don’t tape it. The air gap stops water from being siphoned out.
  • The right depth and height. These differ by brand, so check your installation guide. For example, Whirlpool says no more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe on its front-loaders and no more than 5 inches on top-loaders. Samsung says 6 to 8 inches, at least 18 inches high, and no higher than 96 inches. GE says most of its full-size washers pump up to 8 feet high, or 5 feet for compact models. Samsung also says not to use a drain hose extension kit.
  • Frozen hose (cold laundry rooms or garages). LG says water in the pump or hose can freeze. Its fix is to pour warm water (122 to 140°F) into the drum and wait 1 to 2 hours. LG says not to use boiling water, which can damage parts.

Step 4: Clean the pump filter (most front-loaders)

Many front-loaders have a small access door at the bottom front that covers a pump filter (also called a debris filter) that catches coins, hair ties, and lint. Samsung says top-loaders don’t have a removable pump filter, and GE says front-loaders without a front access door need service for this.

The general steps from LG and Samsung are: unplug the washer, lay down towels and a shallow pan, open the access cover, drain the leftover water through the small drain tube, then twist the filter counterclockwise to remove it and clean it (LG). Open it slowly and keep towels handy. LG says to put the filter back by turning it clockwise until it stops, so it doesn’t leak. Follow your owner’s manual, because the location differs by model. On some Whirlpool models, it’s behind the dispenser drawer.

On Whirlpool front-loaders, also wipe out the rubber door seal: Whirlpool says it has small drain holes that can clog.

Step 5: Too much soap

Whirlpool says too much detergent or non-HE detergent makes excess suds, which can stop the washer from pumping and spinning. LG says suds can trigger its OE code by creating air pockets the pump can’t move. Use HE detergent, measure it, and on LG models run a Tub Clean cycle with less detergent, then try again (LG).

Step 6: Is it the washer or your house?

If the standpipe or floor drain is clogged, it’s a plumbing problem, not a washer problem. Two manufacturer tests:

  • GE: take the hose out of the standpipe and pour a quart of water into the standpipe. If it won’t drain, GE recommends a plumber.
  • LG: take the hose out of the drain (hold it over a bucket or tub) and run a Spin Only cycle. If water pumps out normally, the washer is working, and your home drain may need to be cleared.

When to stop and call a pro

  • The tub is full and the washer just hums. GE says this needs service.
  • No pump sound at all. LG’s test is to run a high-speed Spin Only cycle and listen for a hum in the first 15 seconds. If there’s no hum, LG says the drain pump isn’t working and needs repair.
  • The error code comes back after you’ve cleaned the filter, checked the hose, and run a cleaning cycle.
  • The door stays locked after an unplug reset and Drain & Spin.
  • There’s no front filter access on your front-loader (GE says that model needs service), or you see a cracked pump.
  • A top-loader won’t drain or spin when the lid is closed. GE Factory Service lists a faulty lid switch as a possible cause, and that’s a part replacement.
  • You smell burning or the breaker trips. Unplug it and call a technician.
  • It’s still under warranty or a protection plan. Check that before booking anyone else.

What does it cost?

These are published averages, not quotes. Your price depends on your city, the part, and the diagnostic fee.

  • Whirlpool lists average out-of-warranty washer repairs at $215 to $385 for parts and labor. It says these are 2020 averages from its W Service network in six metro areas (Baltimore, Boston, Orlando, Birmingham, Seattle, and New York) and that actual costs vary (Whirlpool).
  • Sears Home Services says the average washer repair costs $150 to $350 depending on the problem. It’s a repair company, so treat that as a vendor estimate. Sears also says repair is often more cost-effective than replacement if the washer is under 10 years old (Sears Home Services).

A clogged house drain is a plumber’s job, so it won’t be covered by either of these estimates. Ask for a written estimate and whether the diagnostic fee counts toward the repair.

How to find a repair service

Start with the brand. Its service line knows your model and warranty. These links were all checked on November 18, 2026:

  • Whirlpool: online scheduling.
  • GE Appliances: schedule through Bodewell.
  • Samsung: repair request portal, or call 1-800-SAMSUNG.
  • LG: repair request page.
  • Any brand: Sears Home Services says it repairs most major washer brands no matter where you bought them.

Have your model and serial number ready; your owner’s manual shows where the label is on your model. Tell the scheduler about any error code you saw and what you already tried. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm on the linked page before you call.
